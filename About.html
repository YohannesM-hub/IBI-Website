The story behind the seal

International Bible Institute began as a small class of believers meeting part-time in Bole, Addis Ababa, and has grown into a training ground for pastors, teachers, and Christian leaders across Ethiopia — without ever losing sight of why it started.

Our history

From a small class in Bole to a growing institute

IBI's growth has never been about numbers for their own sake — it has followed the churches and communities asking for trained leaders.

January 2013

A part-time class in Bole, Addis Ababa

Pastor Girma Metaferia founded International Bible Institute, teaching a first class of just 10–15 students part-time, with a conviction that ministry training should form the whole person — spirit, mind, and body.

Growth

Beyond Addis Ababa

As word spread through local churches, IBI's teaching reached beyond the capital to Assela, Lege Tafo, Dukem, Bekoji, Bale Robe, and Boditi-Wolayita — carried by faculty willing to travel to where the students already were.

Partnership

Affiliation with Kingdom Bible Seminary

IBI became affiliated with Kingdom Bible Seminary, strengthening its curriculum through shared academic cooperation and a common commitment to sound doctrine and practical ministry formation.

Today

A program built and conferred by IBI

IBI independently confers its own Certificate, Diploma, and Bachelor of Arts credentials, building on the same founding conviction — that ministry training should form the whole person, spirit, mind, and body.

Our approach

Built around the student who's already serving

IBI wasn't designed like a conventional seminary, and that's on purpose.

Bi-vocational by design

Class times that bend around ministry

Day and evening tracks exist because most of our students are already pastoring, teaching, or working while they study — we schedule around calling, not the other way around.

In their own language

Taught in English, Amharic & Afan Oromiffa

Doctrine and theology land differently in a student's heart language. Teaching across three languages is a deliberate choice, not a limitation.

Multiplication, not just instruction

Graduates who go on to teach others

Our field-ministry requirement sends students into local churches while they study, so what's learned in the classroom is tested — and passed on — in real congregations.
